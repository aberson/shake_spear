# Project naming: a sample exchange

This project, its candidates, and the operator's reactions are invented.

Start from the workshop root with `/project-naming` in Claude Code, or tell any
file-reading assistant: "Read `skills/project_naming.md` and help name this
project," followed by the description. No CLI scaffold is needed.

## Brief and batch 1

**Operator:** I'm making a little tool for a shared electronics lab. It tracks
borrowed equipment, records returns, and nudges people when something is due.
The maintainers will use the repo name. Short, two words, dry humor. The funny
part is that the multimeters have a more organized social life than we do.

**Assistant:**

- `loan-officer`
- `bench-clerk`
- `due-process`
- `return-policy`
- `cabinet-minister`
- `borrowed-time`
- `case-worker` — the equipment travels in cases.
- `gentle-reminder`

Which made you laugh, which direction wants more, and which would you use?

## Mixed feedback and batch 2

**Operator:** Cabinet-minister made me laugh, but I wouldn't use it. Due-process
and return-policy are both usable; keep those. More return-something, please.
I like how ordinary it sounds. Loan-officer is too financial. Keep it short.

**Assistant:** I'll keep the two usable names and the ordinary `return-*`
wording. The new ones try a desk job, a visit, paperwork, and travel; the
cabinet joke can retire.

Still in play: `due-process`, `return-policy`.

New contenders:

- `return-desk`
- `return-visit`
- `return-receipt`
- `return-ticket`

Wild card: `shelf-respect`.

Any of these move ahead of the two you're keeping?

**Operator:** Return-receipt. That sounds like the boring little thing I
actually need. That's the name.

**Assistant:** `return-receipt` it is.

## What changed

The laugh did not become a shortlist vote. The two usable names stayed visible
and unchanged. The requested word and matter-of-fact attitude survived, while
the role and implied activity varied. The financial direction was dropped.
The wild card left a small opening for surprise without breaking the length
constraint. Once the operator chose, the assistant stopped.
